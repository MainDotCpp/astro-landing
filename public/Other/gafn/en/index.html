Crypto Fraud Funds Traced

The messages I almost deleted

I sent about US$68,000 in Bitcoin to someone I’d been talking to on Facebook for two months. The day the withdrawal button stopped working, I knew. The first thing the Sullivan & Cromwell LLP told me was to save every message and screenshot the site before I deleted anything — that turned out to be the whole case. They can followed the coins to an exchange account with a real name attached to it.

Impersonation Account Frozen

The “friend” who needed money that same day

A friend messaged me on WhatsApp. He was stuck overseas, needed cash before the banks closed, and sent me an account in a country he’d never mentioned. It was the way he wrote that stopped me. I asked Sullivan & Cromwell to check first. They flagged it to WhatsApp the same day, and the login history showed his account had been taken over three days earlier. The platform froze it by that afternoon.

Romance Scam Stopped in Time

Ten months of messages, from a woman who never existed

We met on a dating app. She said she was working overseas on contract, and for most of a year she never asked me for a thing — until she mentioned the private fund a colleague had put her onto. I’d already sent one payment and was about to send a bigger one. Sullivan & Cromwell ran her photos and her ID: the passport was a digital fake, and the same face turned up on four other profiles.

Fake Job Platform Site Taken Down

The side job that always needed one more deposit

I found it on a job board. Simple work, small commission each time, and the first two rounds actually paid out. Then, to take any of it out, I had to put more in to unlock the next tier. Sullivan & Cromwell went through the order numbers I’d been sent and found the same ones repeating on a loop. The site was running off the same setup as six others they’d already reported.

Investment Fraud Warning Issued

3% a day, right up until the withdrawals stopped

It paid out for six weeks straight. That’s why I put more in, and why my brother did too. Sullivan & Cromwell traced where the deposits were actually going: straight back out to pay the people who’d joined before us. We got the warning into the group chat two days before the next round of deposits was due.

Phishing Domain Blocked

A bank text, a login page, forty seconds

The text landed in the same thread as my real bank alerts. I tapped it, typed my password, and the page just spun. 40 seconds later, my actual bank called me. I sent Sullivan & Cromwell the link that night. They had the domain reported and pulled within a day, then flagged it to two other banks whose customers were getting the same fake page.
